Grammar reminder / Recordatorio de gramática

Both options require Reported Speech + Opinion expressions

Reporting verbs to use:
  • said / told + (that)
  • claimed (unverified)
  • admitted (confessed)
  • promised (committed)
  • announced (public)
  • asked if / wanted to know
Opinion expressions:
  • In my opinion…
  • I think / I believe…
  • I'm not happy about…
  • I'm surprised that…
  • Despite… / However…
  • I feel that…

📰 Option A — Structure & Model
How to structure the press conference + a model example. / Cómo estructurar la rueda de prensa + un ejemplo modelo.
Structure guide / Guía de estructura
1. Journalist opens ~30 sec
  • Introduce the news story — who, what, where
  • Announce who you are interviewing
  • "Good evening. I'm reporting from… where…"
  • "With me today is [politician], who…"
2. Politician's opening statement ⏱ minimum 1 minute
  • State your position clearly
  • Explain what happened from your perspective
  • Use at least one opinion expression
  • Make one promise using reported speech form
  • "Thank you. I want to say that in my opinion…"
  • "I have to admit that… however…"
  • "Despite what has been reported, I…"
  • "I want to promise that…"
3. Q&A — Journalist asks 4 questions ~3 min
  • Journalist: ask questions from the scenario card
  • Politician: answer each one in 2-3 sentences
  • Politician: use "I claimed / I admitted / I promised"
  • "Could you tell us what happened when…?"
  • "What do you say to people who claim that…?"
  • "Are you planning to…? And if so, when?"
  • "What did [other politician / party] say about…?"
4. Journalist's final report — REQUIRED Reported Speech ⏱ minimum 1 minute
  • Report what the politician said using Reported Speech
  • Use at least 3 different reporting verbs
  • Express your own opinion at the end
  • "The politician claimed that…"
  • "She admitted that… but also promised…"
  • "When asked about…, he said that…"
  • "In my opinion, the most important thing was…"
Model example / Ejemplo — Scenario A: The Campaign Fire
Journalist (opening):
"Good evening. I'm reporting live from the campaign headquarters of Mr. James Brown, where a fire broke out earlier today. With me is Mr. Brown himself, who has called this press conference to address the situation."
Politician (opening statement — 1 min+):
"Thank you. I want to be completely clear: I was having lunch when the fire started. I had no idea what was happening until my assistant called me. In my opinion, this was not an accident. I have received personal threats over the past few weeks, and I believe someone tried to destroy my campaign. Despite this, I am not going to resign. I have promised the people of this city that I will fight for free education and for safer streets, and I intend to keep that promise. However, I want to say that I am not happy about the way some newspapers have reported this story. They have claimed things that are simply not true."
Journalist (question):
"Mr. Brown — have the police found any evidence of arson? And are you saying that someone deliberately tried to murder you?"
Politician (answer):
"The police are investigating. They have not confirmed arson yet, but I believe the evidence will show the truth. I am surprised that anyone would question my word on this. I have dedicated my life to this community."
Journalist (final report — 1 min+):
"This has been a remarkable press conference. Mr. Brown claimed that he was not present when the fire began — he said he had been having lunch at the time. He admitted that he had received personal threats, and he insisted that this was not an accident. When I asked him if the police had found evidence, he said they were still investigating. He promised that he would not resign and that he would continue to fight for free education. However, he did not answer my question about whether he believed someone had tried to murder him. In my opinion, the most interesting moment was when he said he was not happy about the press coverage. I think there are still many questions that remain unanswered tonight."

Notice: Journalist's final report has 5 reported speech sentences · 3 reporting verbs (claimed, admitted, promised, insisted, said) · 1 opinion expression · over 1 minute speaking

📺 Option B — The News Reporter
Individual: you are a TV journalist delivering a political news report. Minimum 1 minute of continuous speaking. / Individual: eres un/a periodista de televisión que presenta un reporte de noticias políticas. Mínimo 1 minuto de habla continua.
What you do / Qué haces

Prepare a 1–2 minute TV news report on a political story

You choose one of the scenarios below OR use a real news story from your country. You must report what politicians or officials said using Reported Speech, and give your own opinion at the end.

Eliges uno de los escenarios abajo O usas una noticia real de tu país. Debes reportar lo que dijeron políticos o funcionarios usando Estilo Indirecto, y dar tu opinión al final.

Choose a story / Elige una historia

1Election Night Results

A local election just ended. Report the results and what the candidates said.
Your report must include:
  • Who won and who was defeated
  • The key issues of the campaign
  • What the winner said (Reported Speech)
  • What the defeated candidate said (Reported Speech)
  • Your opinion on the results
Useful phrases:
  • "The Liberal Party candidate announced that…"
  • "The defeated candidate claimed that…"
  • "Voters said they had chosen [name] because…"
  • "The key issues were… and the winner promised…"

2Political Scandal

A politician has been caught in a scandal. Report the story and reactions.
Your report must include:
  • What the politician did (the scandal)
  • What the police or investigators said
  • What the politician admitted or claimed
  • What their party leader said about it
  • Your opinion: should they resign?
Useful phrases:
  • "Officials announced that…"
  • "The politician admitted that… but also claimed…"
  • "Despite the scandal, [name] promised…"
  • "In my opinion, this situation shows that…"

3A Real News Story from Colombia

Use a real political event you know about — local, national, or international.
Your report must include:
  • A clear summary of what happened (who, what, where, when)
  • At least 2 Reported Speech sentences from real quotes
  • The public or political reaction
  • Your personal opinion on the event
Useful phrases:
  • "The president/mayor/candidate said that…"
  • "Officials claimed / admitted / announced that…"
  • "I believe this is important because…"
  • "In my opinion, the government should…"
📺 Option B — Structure & Model
How to structure your 1–2 minute news report + a model example. / Cómo estructurar tu reporte de 1–2 minutos + un ejemplo modelo.
Structure / Estructura

Use all 5 parts. This structure guarantees you speak for over 1 minute. / Usa las 5 partes. Esta estructura garantiza que hablas más de 1 minuto.

1.Opening — who, what, where~15 sec
  • "Good evening. This is [your name] reporting for Channel News."
  • "Tonight's top story: [headline — 1 sentence]."
  • "I'm reporting from [place], where [brief summary]."
2.The story — what happened~20 sec
  • Explain the background: what happened, when, and why it matters
  • Use Past Simple and Past Perfect to narrate the sequence
  • "Earlier today… / Last night… / Following reports that…"
3.What they said — Reported Speech ⭐ REQUIRED~20 sec
  • Report at least 3 things that were said — use different reporting verbs
  • "[Name] claimed that… / admitted that… / promised that…"
  • "When asked about [topic], [name] said that…"
  • "Officials announced that… / The candidate told reporters that…"
4.Reactions — what others said~10 sec
  • "Members of the opposition claimed that…"
  • "Citizens expressed surprise, saying they had not expected…"
  • "The party leader told reporters that…"
5.Your opinion + closing~15 sec
  • "In my opinion, this story is important because…"
  • "I think / I believe the most significant fact is…"
  • "Despite what officials said, I feel that…"
  • "This is [your name], reporting for Channel News. Good night."
Model report / Ejemplo — Scenario 1: Election Night
"Good evening. This is María Rodríguez reporting for Channel 5 News. Tonight's top story: the results of the local election are in, and the Liberal Party has won by a large majority. I'm reporting from the Liberal Party's headquarters, where supporters have been celebrating since 9 pm.

The Liberal candidate, Rosa Morales, won 58% of the vote. The Conservative candidate, Mr. Torres, was defeated with only 34%. The key issues in this campaign were education, crime, and unemployment.

Following the results, Rosa Morales held a brief press conference. She announced that she was honored by the trust of the voters. She promised that her first priority would be to improve public schools in the city. She also said that she had not expected such a large majority, and that she was surprised and grateful.

Meanwhile, the defeated candidate, Mr. Torres, told reporters that he accepted the results but claimed that some of the counting had been irregular. He said he was disappointed and that he would speak to his team before making any further comments.

In my opinion, this result shows that voters in this city care deeply about education. Despite what Mr. Torres claimed about the counting, I believe the result is clear and fair. I think the new city council has a lot of work to do, and I hope they keep their promises.

This is María Rodríguez, reporting for Channel 5 News. Good night."

Notice: 5 Reported Speech sentences · 4 reporting verbs (announced, promised, said, claimed, told) · 2 opinion expressions · clear 5-part structure · well over 1 minute speaking
