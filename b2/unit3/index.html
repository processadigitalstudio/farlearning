What Are Non-Restrictive Relative Clauses?

A non-restrictive relative clause adds extra information about a noun — but the sentence still makes complete sense without it. These clauses are always separated by commas.

My manager, who has worked here for twenty years, is retiring next month.
(We already know which manager — the clause just adds extra information.)

Compare this with a restrictive clause (no commas), which identifies which person or thing we mean:

The manager who hired me is retiring next month.
(Without the clause, we don't know which manager.)

Relative Pronouns in Non-Restrictive Clauses

PronounRefers toExample
who people My colleague, who sits next to me, got promoted.
which things / ideas The report, which took three weeks, was rejected.
where places The conference room, where we always meet, is being renovated.
whose possession (people/things) The HR director, whose office is on the 5th floor, called me in.

Note: In non-restrictive clauses, that is NOT used. You must use who or which.

The meeting, that was scheduled for Monday, was cancelled.
✓ The meeting, which was scheduled for Monday, was cancelled.

Comma Rules

  • Always use a comma before the relative pronoun.
  • If the clause is in the middle of the sentence, use commas on both sides.
  • If the clause is at the end, only one comma before it is needed.
Middle: The CEO, who resigned last week, was very popular.
End: We filed the complaint with HR, which took two weeks to respond.

Non-Restrictive vs Restrictive — Key Test

Ask yourself: "Can I remove this clause and still know exactly what the sentence is about?"

  • Yes → Non-Restrictive → use commas
  • No → Restrictive → no commas
✓ My supervisor, who always arrives late, never checks our work.
(One supervisor — the clause is just extra information)

✓ The supervisor who always arrives late never checks our work.
(Multiple supervisors — the clause tells us which one)

Common Uses at Work

  • Adding background on a person: Our union rep, who has 15 years of experience, advised us to file a grievance.
  • Adding context about an event: The layoff announcement, which shocked everyone, came on a Friday.
  • Describing a place: The break room, where we held our meeting, was too small.
  • Showing possession: The colleague, whose contract was not renewed, is taking the company to arbitration.
Dialogue 1 — The News (Layoffs Announced)
Elena: Did you hear? The management, who met all weekend, just announced thirty layoffs.
Paul: Thirty? The company, which posted record profits last year, is laying people off?
Elena: That's what HR said. Our department, where the most junior staff work, is affected.
Paul: I'm calling the union. Our rep, whose number I have, needs to know immediately.
Elena: Good idea. The workers, who were given no warning, have a right to representation.
Dialogue 2 — Fifty Days and Counting (Strike Talk)
Marco: The strike, which started fifty days ago, shows no sign of ending.
Diane: The workers, who have been without pay for weeks, are under enormous pressure.
Marco: Management offered a settlement, which the union rejected last Thursday.
Diane: The mediator, whose proposal seemed fair to both sides, is coming back tomorrow.
Marco: Let's hope so. Collective bargaining, which is a legal right, must be respected.
Dialogue 3 — Quitting (Workplace Complaint)
Sofia: I've decided to resign. My manager, who has been harassing me for months, crossed a line.
HR: That's very serious. The HR office, where all complaints are handled confidentially, is here to help.
Sofia: I already filed a grievance, which was ignored for three weeks. Nothing changed.
HR: I understand. The process, which can feel slow, does protect you legally. Please don't resign yet.
Sofia: The company, whose reputation I care about, needs to do better. I'll give it one more week.

1. Which sentence uses commas correctly for a non-restrictive clause?

Option B is correct. In a non-restrictive clause, commas appear before and after the clause when it is in the middle of the sentence. Option A (no commas) is restrictive. Option C is missing the closing comma.

2. Choose the correct word: "The conference room, ___ we hold all our meetings, is being repainted."

"Where" is used for places. The conference room is a place, so "where" is correct. "Which" is for things (not for location), and "who" is for people.

3. An employee who reports illegal behavior inside a company is called a…

A "whistleblower" exposes wrongdoing inside an organization. A "mediator" helps resolve disputes. An "arbitrator" makes binding decisions in disputes.

4. Identify the non-restrictive clause in this sentence: "The layoff announcement, which shocked everyone, came on a Friday."

"which shocked everyone" is the non-restrictive relative clause — it is separated by commas and adds extra information about the announcement. Remove it and the main sentence "The layoff announcement came on a Friday" still works perfectly.

5. Money paid to a worker when they are dismissed for no fault of their own is called…

"Severance pay" is financial compensation given to a worker when they lose their job through no fault of their own, such as in a layoff.

6. Which is a restrictive clause (no commas needed)?

Option B is restrictive — we don't know which manager until the clause "who approved my vacation request" identifies them. Options A and C are non-restrictive and correctly use commas.

7. "To delegate" means…

To "delegate" is to assign tasks or authority to another person, usually someone below you in a hierarchy. It is a key management skill.

Peter Gibbons, a bored software engineer at Initech, hates his job, his micromanaging boss, and the meaningless corporate culture around him.
