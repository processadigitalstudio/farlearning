🎤 Speaking Exercise

Unit 3: Problems at Work  ·  Non-Restrictive Relative Clauses

Instructions for Option A
Work in pairs. Choose one scenario. Each person takes one role. Discuss the workplace problem for 3–5 minutes. Try to use non-restrictive relative clauses (who / which / where / whose + commas) and at least 4 vocabulary words from the unit.
Choose a Scenario
1
The Unfair Layoff
Employee A: Worker at risk  ·  Employee B: Union representative
The company has announced twenty redundancies. Employee A believes the selection process was unfair and wants to file a grievance. Employee B is the union rep who will listen, advise, and explain the options (arbitration, legal action, negotiation).
  • What happened and why does A feel it was unfair?
  • What does the union advise? What are the steps?
  • What is A's main concern — money, reputation, or staying at the company?
Try using: "My manager, who has always favored certain people, chose who to lay off."
2
Burnout at the Office
Employee A: Exhausted team member  ·  Employee B: Team leader / Manager
The team has been understaffed for three months. Employee A is overworked and burned out, missing deadlines. They need to tell their manager, who must listen and decide how to delegate work differently without losing productivity.
  • How does A describe the situation? What evidence do they give?
  • What solutions can B offer? (hire temp staff, delegate, reduce scope)
  • Do they reach an agreement by the end?
Try using: "The project, which has three times more tasks than usual, is destroying the team's morale."
3
The Whistleblower Dilemma
Employee A: Employee who discovered misconduct  ·  Employee B: Trusted colleague
Employee A has discovered that a manager is falsifying expense reports. They are afraid of retaliation but feel it is wrong to stay silent. Employee B must advise — should A report it to HR? Who can protect them? What are the risks?
  • What exactly did A discover, and how?
  • What are the risks of speaking up vs. staying silent?
  • Does A decide to report it in the end?
Try using: "The HR department, where all complaints are supposed to be confidential, might not protect me."
4
The Strike Decision
Employee A: Union member in favor of striking  ·  Employee B: Union member against striking
The union is voting on whether to go on strike. Management, which has offered a 2% pay rise, is calling it fair. The union, whose members are divided, must decide. A wants to strike; B wants to keep negotiating. Debate the decision.
  • What are A's strongest arguments for striking?
  • What are B's strongest arguments for continuing to negotiate?
  • What would make each person change their mind?
Try using: "The offer, which amounts to a pay cut in real terms, is not acceptable."

HR Formal Complaint — Individual Speaking Task

You are attending a formal HR meeting to present a workplace complaint. You will speak for 1.5 to 2 minutes, describing the problem, the people involved, and what you are asking the company to do.

Choose your character below. Each character has a different complaint. Use non-restrictive relative clauses and at least 5 vocabulary words from the unit in your presentation.

Step 1 — Choose Your Character (tap to see your situation)
Alex Merritt
Senior Analyst, Finance Dept.
Your workload tripled after three colleagues were laid off. You have been overworked for six months and are now experiencing burnout. Your manager, who promised to hire replacements, has done nothing. You want the company to hire two new analysts or reduce your caseload immediately.
Diana Fuentes
Project Coordinator, Marketing
Your supervisor has been making dismissive comments about your work in front of colleagues for months. You filed a grievance three weeks ago, which was never acknowledged. You believe this is a form of workplace harassment. You are asking HR to investigate and issue a formal reprimand.
James Okonkwo
Software Developer, IT
You discovered that your department head has been approving fake invoices. You are a whistleblower, which puts you in a vulnerable position. You are asking HR to confirm your legal protection before you give a formal statement. You also need to know about severance pay in case you are retaliated against.
Laura Svensson
Customer Service Lead
You were passed over for a promotion — the role went to someone less qualified, whose friendship with the regional director seems to have been the deciding factor. You have three years of excellent performance reviews, which should have qualified you automatically. You are demanding a transparent process.
Miguel Rios
Warehouse Supervisor
The warehouse, where your team works, has serious safety violations that you have reported twice. Management, which has ignored your reports, is now pressuring you to sign off on inspections you did not carry out. You are refusing and asking HR to escalate the matter to the regional director immediately.
Nina Hartmann
HR Coordinator, People Ops
You are resigning due to a toxic work culture, but before leaving you want to document a pattern of misconduct by the department VP. You have gathered written evidence, which you will submit today. You are asking the company to launch a formal investigation and protect your identity as a source.
Step 2 — Structure Your Presentation (1.5 – 2 minutes)
1
Introduce yourself and the purpose (15 sec) State your name, your role, and why you requested this HR meeting.
"My name is [character], and I am here today to formally report..."
2
Describe the problem with detail (40 sec) Explain what happened, who was involved, and for how long. Use non-restrictive relative clauses to add context about people and events.
"My manager, who has been with the company for eight years, began..."
3
Explain the impact on you (25 sec) How has this affected your work, health, or professional reputation? Use emotional and professional language.
"This situation, which has lasted for over three months, has led to..."
4
State your request clearly (20 sec) What do you want HR to do? Be specific — investigation, reprimand, compensation, policy change?
"I am formally requesting that the company... within the next two weeks."
Language Bank — Useful Phrases

Opening

  • I am here today to formally report…
  • I would like to bring to your attention…
  • This meeting concerns a serious matter…

Describing the problem

  • This situation, which began in…, has…
  • My colleague, who is responsible for…
  • The department, where I work daily…

Explaining impact

  • As a result of this, I have been unable to…
  • This has had a significant effect on…
  • I have documented instances, which show…

Making a request

  • I am formally requesting a full investigation.
  • I expect a written response within…
  • I am also asking for confirmation of…
Self-Check Before You Speak
  • I know my character's name, job, and complaint.
  • I have planned at least 2 non-restrictive relative clauses (with commas).
  • I will use at least 5 vocabulary words from Unit 3.
  • I know what I am asking HR to do (my request).
  • I will speak for 1.5 to 2 minutes (not read from notes).
  • I will stay in character and use formal, professional language.
