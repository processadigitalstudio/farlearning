Grammar: Present Perfect

Actions that started in the past and are still relevant now — or life experiences.

1. Form — Statements

Subject + have / has + past participle
  • I have studied English for three years. Todavía sigo estudiando.
  • She has worked at that school since 2020.
  • They have finished the exam.

2. Form — Questions & Short Answers

Have / Has + subject + past participle + …?
  • Have you taken the math test? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
  • Has she graduated yet? — Yes, she has.
  • Where have you studied before?

3. Present Perfect vs. Past Simple

  • Present Perfect: connects past to present. No specific time. "I have visited Paris." (at some point in my life)
  • Past Simple: finished action, specific time. "I visited Paris in 2019." (specific moment)

4. Time Markers

ever never already yet just for + period since + point so far recently

Dialogue 1 — Math Test
Two students are talking about their math test.
Ann:Hi Bob. Have you taken the math test yet?
Bob:Yes, I have. I took it yesterday.
Ann:How was it? Was it difficult?
Bob:Well, I'm not sure. Some questions were easy, but others were really hard.
Ann:Really? I'm nervous. I haven't studied enough.
Bob:Don't worry. You'll do fine — you're a good student.
Dialogue 2 — The Job Interview
Karen is being interviewed for a job in the advertising department.
Interviewer:So, Karen — tell me, have you ever worked in advertising before?
Karen:Yes, I have. I worked at a small agency for two years after university.
Interviewer:And what have you done since then?
Karen:I have been freelancing. I have designed campaigns for three different clients.
Interviewer:Interesting. Have you ever managed a team?
Karen:Not officially, but I have led several projects.
