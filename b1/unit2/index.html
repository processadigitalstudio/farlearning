Grammar: Present Perfect Progressive

For continuous or ongoing actions that started in the past and are still happening — or have just stopped.

1. Form — Statements

Subject + have / has been + verb-ing
  • I have been living in London for six months. Empecé hace 6 meses y sigo allí.
  • She has been studying French since January.
  • They have been travelling around Europe.

2. Form — Questions

Have / Has + subject + been + verb-ing …?
  • Have you been waiting long? — Yes, for 20 minutes.
  • How long has she been living abroad?
  • What have you been doing lately?

3. Present Perfect vs. Present Perfect Progressive

  • Present Perfect: focuses on the result. "I have read three books." (completed / how many)
  • Present Perfect Progressive: focuses on the activity and its duration. "I have been reading for two hours." (ongoing / how long)

4. Common Time Markers

for + period since + point all day lately recently how long?

Dialogues

Dialogue 1 — Enjoying Your Stay
A woman is at a barbeque, asking another woman about her time in the country.
Sarah:Hi! You must be Yuki. I'm Sarah. Welcome!
Yuki:Thank you. It's very nice to meet you.
Sarah:So, how long have you been staying here in the US?
Yuki:I have been living with my host family for about three months now.
Sarah:Oh wow. And how have you been enjoying it?
Yuki:I love it! I have been practising English every day, and I have been meeting so many new people.
Dialogue 2 — Airmail to Tokyo
A woman at the post office is asking the clerk questions about sending an airmail package.
Customer:Hi, I'd like to send this package to Tokyo, please.
Clerk:Sure. Airmail or surface mail?
Customer:Airmail, please. How long does it take?
Clerk:About 7 to 10 business days. Let me weigh it… That'll be $32.50.
Customer:OK. And how have the shipping rates been changing lately?
Clerk:They have been going up a lot, actually. Especially for international packages.
