Grammar: Passive Voice

Used when the action matters more than who does it — perfect for traditions and celebrations.

1. Form — Passive Voice

Subject + be (any tense) + past participle (+ by + agent)
  • The turkey is cooked by my grandmother every year. (present)
  • The parade was held on December 7th. (past)
  • The decorations will be put up tomorrow. (future)
  • The invitations have been sent. (present perfect)

2. With or Without Agent

  • With agent: The cake was made by my mom. Use "by" when the doer is important.
  • Without agent: The cake was made yesterday. Skip "by" when the doer is unknown or obvious.
  • The streets are decorated every December. (Who? Everyone, it's not important.)

3. Passive Modals

Subject + modal + be + past participle
  • The food must be prepared early. (obligation)
  • Decorations should be taken down after January 6th. (advice)
  • Invitations can be sent by email. (possibility)
  • The music has to be loud! (necessity)

4. When to Use Passive

  • The doer is unknown: "My wallet was stolen."
  • The doer is obvious: "The criminal was arrested." (by the police, obviously)
  • The doer is unimportant: "Buñuelos are made during Christmas."
  • You want to focus on the action, not who did it.

Dialogue 1 — The First Thanksgiving
Andrew describes the American holiday Thanksgiving to his friend Kenji.
Kenji:So Andrew, what exactly is Thanksgiving?
Andrew:It's a holiday that is celebrated on the fourth Thursday of November. Families get together to eat.
Kenji:What is eaten at Thanksgiving?
Andrew:Turkey, mostly! It is served with mashed potatoes, stuffing, cranberry sauce, and pumpkin pie for dessert.
Kenji:And where was the first Thanksgiving held?
Andrew:It was celebrated in 1621 by the Pilgrims and Native Americans. A big parade is also organized every year in New York City.
Dialogue 2 — Sorry I'm Late
A man at a party is apologizing to a woman for being late.
Mark:Lisa, I'm so sorry I'm late!
Lisa:It's okay, Mark. What happened?
Mark:My car was blocked in the parking lot. And then the main street was closed because of a parade.
Lisa:Oh no! Well, don't worry — the food hasn't been served yet.
Mark:Great! And look, I brought this — it was made by my mother. A traditional cake.
Lisa:That's so sweet! It will be enjoyed by everyone, I'm sure.
