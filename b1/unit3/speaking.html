🎤 SPEAKING EXERCISE · UNIT 03

Celebration Reporters

Two fun options — one individual (be a reporter!), one in pairs (invent a wild celebration). Both practise passive voice.

THE TASK

📺 My Country's Weirdest Celebration

You are an international TV reporter. Your job is to describe a Colombian celebration to foreigners who have never heard of it — making it sound as exotic and interesting as possible.

Speak for at least 1 minute. Use passive voice to describe:

  • Where and when it is celebrated.
  • What is eaten or drunk.
  • What is worn or how people are dressed.
  • What is done during the celebration (music, dancing, parades).
  • Why it is loved by Colombians.

EXAMPLE

LAURA — 1 MIN — NOCHE DE LAS VELITAS
"Good evening, viewers around the world! Tonight I am in Colombia for one of its most beautiful traditions: Noche de las Velitas, or 'Night of the Little Candles'.

This magical night is celebrated every year on December 7th, and it marks the beginning of Christmas season. Thousands of candles and paper lanterns are lit in the streets, on balconies, and in parks. Entire neighborhoods are transformed into golden rivers of light.

Traditional food is shared with neighbors — buñuelos, natilla, and hot chocolate are enjoyed by everyone. In cities like Medellín, massive light displays are installed along the river, and parades are held all week.

This celebration is loved because it brings families and neighbors together before Christmas. Trust me — if you have never seen Colombia on December 7th, you have not seen the real Colombia. Back to you in the studio!"

🕯️ NOCHE DE LAS VELITASDecember 7th — candles everywhere, start of Christmas.
🎭 CARNAVAL DE BARRANQUILLAFebruary/March — the biggest party in Colombia.
🎆 AÑO VIEJODec 31st — burning the "old year" doll, 12 grapes, yellow underwear.
🌸 FERIA DE LAS FLORESAugust in Medellín — flower parades and silleteros.
🙏 SEMANA SANTAHoly Week in Popayán — processions, no meat, deep tradition.
👑 REINADO DE BELLEZABeauty pageants in Cartagena — contests and parties everywhere.
⚽ COPA AMÉRICASoccer celebrations — yellow jerseys, honking cars, flags.
🎊 FESTIVAL VALLENATOApril in Valledupar — accordion music, dancing, legends.

USEFUL SENTENCE STARTERS

  • "This celebration is held every year on ______."
  • "Traditional food like ______ is served."
  • "People are dressed in ______."
  • "Fireworks are launched at midnight."
  • "Music is played in every street."
  • "This tradition has been celebrated for over 100 years."
  • "It is loved by Colombians because ______."

THE TASK

🕵️ The Mystery Celebration

In pairs (2 students only). One student is a local from a totally invented country, inventing a totally fake celebration. The other is a skeptical journalist trying to figure out if the celebration is real or made up.

The journalist must ask at least 5 questions using passive voice. The local must give creative, detailed answers using passive voice too — and never break character!

Total time: 3–4 minutes per pair. The crazier, the better! 🎉

PICK YOUR FAKE COUNTRY

🧦 SOCKONIAFamous for its "Festival of the Lost Socks". People wear mismatched socks on their heads.
🥔 POTATOLANDCelebrates "National Potato Appreciation Day" with potato thrones.
🐸 FROGISTAN"The Great Frog Race" — amphibians are decorated and raced through town.
☕ COFFEEBEANIA"Caffeine Festival" — people stay awake for 3 days drinking coffee.
🧢 HATOVIAEveryone must wear a different hat every hour of the celebration.
🥒 PICKLELAND"Pickle Parade" — floats made entirely of giant pickles.

EXAMPLE — SOCKONIA / FESTIVAL OF THE LOST SOCKS

DANIELA (JOURNALIST) & SEBASTIÁN (LOCAL) — 3 MIN
Daniela:Good evening, Sebastián. I've heard your country has a very unusual celebration. Can you tell our viewers more?
Sebastián:Of course! Welcome to Sockonia. Every July, our great "Festival of the Lost Socks" is celebrated here.
Daniela:The festival of what? When exactly is it held?
Sebastián:It is held every July 23rd. It began 200 years ago when our king lost his favorite sock in a lake. The sock was never found, so we honor it every year.
Daniela:That's… interesting. And what is worn by people during the festival?
Sebastián:Mismatched socks are worn on our heads — one blue, one red. It's considered bad luck if both socks are matched.
Daniela:And what is eaten during this festival?
Sebastián:Sock-shaped bread is baked by every family. And sock-shaped cookies are given to children. The traditional drink is called "sockijuice".
Daniela:Sockijuice?! What is that made of?
Sebastián:That's a secret. Only our grandmothers know. The recipe has been passed from generation to generation for 200 years.
Daniela:And finally — is this real or are you making it all up?
Sebastián:Everything I have said can be verified at the Sockonia National Museum. Come visit us this July!

USEFUL QUESTIONS FOR THE JOURNALIST

  • When is this celebration held?
  • What is eaten / drunk during the festival?
  • What is worn by people?
  • How are people dressed?
  • Why is this tradition celebrated?
  • Where was this festival started?
  • How long has this tradition been celebrated?
  • What must be done / can't be done during the festival?

USEFUL ANSWERS FOR THE LOCAL

  • "This celebration is held every ______."
  • "______ is eaten only during this festival."
  • "Our clothes are made from ______."
  • "The tradition was started in the year ______."
  • "Dances are performed at midnight."
  • "It is believed that ______ brings good luck."
  • "This custom has been passed down for generations."
